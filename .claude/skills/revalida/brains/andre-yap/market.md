## How to use this file

- These numbers are data. Cite them as data, with the report and period: "PH startups raised $81M in H1 2026 (Kickstart Ventures and DealStreetAsia, Oct 2026)."
- Never put a line from this file in quotation marks, and never attach a source id to it. The S-ids belong to Andre's own corpus. A market figure quoted as Andre is a lie.
- Never say Andre believes a number. Say the number, then give the strategic read in his voice.
- Always name the period. Most figures are half-year, annual, or a forecast, and a 2025 number is not a 2026 fact.
- If a figure is not here, say the brain has no number for it. Do not estimate and present it as data.
- Sources below disagree in places. Where they do, the reason is a definition difference, and the file says so. Give the range and the definition rather than picking the flattering end.

## Venture funding, Southeast Asia

- **$7.25B across 217 equity deals in H1 2026**, the strongest half since H1 2022, but deal volume at its lowest since at least 2018. A single deal, DayOne's $4.5B Series C, lifted the total. (Kickstart Ventures and DealStreetAsia, Southeast Asia Startup Funding Report H1 2026, released 24 Sep 2026)
- A second tracker puts the same half at **$7.4B across 127 rounds**, up 130% year on year, with late stage up 200% to $6.0B and seed up 68% to $328M. Different counting method, same direction: fewer deals, bigger cheques. (Tracxn, SEA Tech H1 2026)
- **Late stage was $5.88B across 23 deals**; early stage $1.72B, up 56.4%; the **median Series A fell to $8M from $11.6M** in late 2025. (Kickstart and DealStreetAsia, H1 2026)
- **12 rounds of $100M or more**, up from 4 in H2 2025. The five largest rounds were 75.5% of all regional funding, leaving about $800M for the other 196 deals. (Tracxn; The Business Times, 24 Sep 2026)

## Where the capital concentrates

- **Singapore took 92% of SEA funding** ($6.7B across 145 of 217 deals). Another count puts it at 94%, or $6.9B of city-level funding. (The Business Times, 24 Sep 2026; Tracxn, H1 2026)
- Outside Singapore the totals rest on single deals: Vietnam $340M across 10 deals with one round at 75% of it; Malaysia the widest at 27 deals for $203M; **the Philippines $80M with one company (Salmon) at 75%**. (The Business Times, 24 Sep 2026)
- AI-focused companies captured **80% of private deployment in the last six months**, up from 30% in H2 2024. (e-Conomy SEA 2026, Google, Temasek, Bain, 21 Oct 2026)

## Venture funding, Philippines

- **$81M across 7 deals in H1 2026**, up from $33M in H2 2025, with deal count the lowest since 2019. That is **1.1% of SEA funding value and 3.2% of deal volume**. (Kickstart and DealStreetAsia, H1 2026, reported 14 to 17 Oct 2026)
- **Fintech took about $61M, roughly 75% of disclosed funding.** Healthcare produced the country's first late-stage deal since H2 2024. (ibid.)
- **January to October 2026: $93M across 6 equity rounds**, up 65% on the same period in 2025 ($56.4M across 17 rounds). Fewer, larger cheques. (Tracxn, Philippines, Oct 2026)
- **About 1,101 funded companies have raised $26.6B cumulatively, with 3 unicorns.** (Tracxn, Philippines, Oct 2026)
- **2025 was a record $1.5B of private capital, up 34%**, across 72 deals, average deal size $20.8M, and the Philippines took **19% of SEA private capital deployment versus 5% in 2020**. This headline conflicts with the 2026 equity numbers above because it counts private capital including debt, not equity rounds only. Use it for direction, not for comparison with the $81M figure. (Foxmont Capital, reported by Manila Bulletin, 24 Mar 2026)
- **Private capital is about 0.3% of Philippine GDP.** (Foxmont, Mar 2026)
- Foxmont plans to deploy up to **PHP 4B** into Philippine startups over the next few years, focused on agriculture, health technology, and heavy manufacturing. (Manila Bulletin, 24 Mar 2026)

## Digital economy

- **Southeast Asia: $368B GMV in 2026, up 17%**, tracking past **$1T by 2035**. E-commerce alone is $226B. (e-Conomy SEA 2026, 21 Oct 2026)
- **Philippines: $36B GMV in 2025, up 16%**, one of the fastest growth rates in the region but from a small base. E-commerce was $24B, over 60% of it; online travel $4B. (e-Conomy SEA 2025, reported Nov 2025)
- **Philippines 2030 forecast: $70B to $140B GMV**, cut from the previous year's $80B to $150B forecast. (e-Conomy SEA 2025)
- Video commerce is about a quarter of Philippine e-commerce GMV, with roughly **475,000 sellers and stores** and 1.2B transactions in 2025. (ibid.)
- Official statistics: the digital economy was **PHP 2.74T of gross value added in 2025, 9.8% of GDP, growing 5.4%**, and employed **10.39M people, 21.2% of total employment**. (Philippine Statistics Authority, Digital Economy Satellite Account, 30 Apr 2026)

## AI adoption

The headline numbers disagree by an order of magnitude because the definitions differ. Give the range, not one number.

- **32% of businesses use AI, up from 21% a year earlier**, a 52% increase, or more than 396,000 businesses. Tech leads at 49%, financial services 45%, professional services 40%. **12% have reached advanced use** (custom systems, multiple models, agentic), up from 8%, and startups lead that at 36%. Only **19% feel ready for agentic AI**. (AWS, "Unlocking the Philippines' AI Potential 2026", with Strand Partners, 1,000 business leaders, reported 15 Oct 2026)
- **14.9% of firms use AI tools, and adoption across industries is 3%**, against 90.8% owning computers and 81% with internet. (Philippine Institute for Development Studies, reported Jul 2026)
- **92% of organisations used AI in some form in 2025, but 65% are still at pilot.** (Swarm, Philippine AI Report 2025, 175 organisations, reported Mar 2026)
- **86% of CEOs have put AI in their strategic plans and 79% have started implementing**, up from 68% and 60% in 2025. Only 43% think their AI investment is sufficient and 40% think they can attract AI talent. (PwC Philippines CEO Survey 2026, 176 leaders)
- Regionally, **243M people in SEA-6 use generative AI, 58% of the working population**, but only **1% to 2% pay for it**, and per-capita use is about a tenth of US levels. The Philippines and Indonesia have the most headroom. (e-Conomy SEA 2026)

## Macro

- **GDP growth 3.4% in 2026 (IMF) and 3.7% (World Bank)**, down from 4.4% in 2025, recovering to **5.1% to 5.2% in 2027**. Q2 2026 grew 2.3%, the weakest since the pandemic; H1 averaged 2.6%, below the government's 3.5% to 4.5% target. (IMF Article IV and WEO, Sep to Oct 2026; World Bank East Asia and Pacific Update, Oct 2026)
- **Inflation 5.6% to 5.8% in 2026**, easing to about 4% in 2027. Driven by energy imports and food. (ibid.)
- The Philippines is described as the regional outlier on energy exposure, and as unable to capture much of the global AI investment boom. (World Bank, Oct 2026)
- **IT-BPM: about $40B in export revenue and 1.9M workers in 2025**, with 67% of surveyed member firms using AI tools. (US International Trade Administration, Jul 2026)

## What this means for the lens (analysis, not data)

This section is a reading of the figures above. It is not Andre's words and must not be quoted as his.

- The 100X terrain in the Philippines is not venture-funded software. At $81M of equity in a half year, the equity market cannot fund a 100X outcome. The capital is in services, IT-BPM, remittances, and increasingly AI-enabled delivery.
- The country has adoption without depth: high intent, low spend, few advanced deployments. That is the definition of underpriced terrain, and also the definition of a market that will not pay much yet.
- Capital concentration in Singapore means a Philippine venture has to be fundable from Singapore or not at all. Build for the region, prove it in Manila.
- Regional AI money is going into infrastructure, not applications. An application-layer 100X thesis has to explain why it wins when the compute owner can copy it.
